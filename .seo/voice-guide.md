## 1. Voice in one paragraph

Bryan writes like a working practitioner arguing with the industry over a beer. He's plainspoken, opinionated, and a little contrarian, and he puts "I" front and center. He opens with something concrete: a link, a quote, a thing that happened at work today, a hot take. Then he gives his read on it in short declarative sentences and lands on a punchy one-liner. His jokes are dry and come from analogies, mostly food, sports, cooking, and Silicon Valley lore ("hog butchering", "omakase designers", "crock pot software", "empty calories", "Be Jungle Jim"). He's skeptical of hype, AI zealotry, roadmaps, LGTM culture, and teams that think they're special. He's enthusiastic about craft, CSS, the browser, Cursor, reading the code, following a methodology "by the book," and one skilled person shipping fast. He sounds like an experienced craftsman from Newport, Kentucky, not a coastal thought leader or a marketing department. His words, from his blog's About page: his "perspectives tend to counter those of his peers on the coasts."

## 2. Traits, with evidence

### Sentence length and rhythm
- On the blog, a typical sentence runs about 18 words (median 18 across his own amoebaunlimited sentences, quotes excluded). He mixes medium sentences with very short punches. Paragraphs run 1 to 4 sentences, and one-sentence paragraphs are common.
- The pattern: set up, then a short pivot ("But that ignores everything that came before AI."), then a payoff line.
- Fragments and stacked short lines for effect: "You won't write the code. / You won't review the code. / You won't read the code." ("The times aren't a changin")
- He ends with a punchline sentence, often one that calls back to the title: "So, no, AI didn't kill Airtable...poor bets did." / "The man-month is dead. Long live the man-day."

### Person and pronouns
- Heavy first person singular: "I think", "I'd wager", "I'm a scrum purist", "I owe my career to Chris Coyier".
- "We" means his team or the industry ("We believe that because we can, we should.").
- He addresses the reader as "you" directly ("Your company is not going to win because you have a novel way of delivering software."). On X, "you" is often a challenge ("Your customers will tell you what you want, you just have to listen!!").
- The amoeba.design offer copy is first person singular, not a corporate "we": "I'll ship polished UI as PRs for your engineering team to review."

### Formality
- Conversational with a light layer of profanity: "bite them in the ass", "flaming asshole", "everyone else is fuckin toast", "your scrum team is fucked", "Hell, I'd even get behind waterfall". There's more of it on X than on the blog, and none on amoeba.design.
- Casual connectors and asides: "Anyways,", "Mind you,", "Don't get me wrong,", "Sure,", "Aggressive? Certainly.", "I didn't think so."
- Southern and Midwestern folksiness: "sic Cursor on 'em", "copypasta voodoo", "bubblegum, duck tape, and Airtable", "hope and gasoline", "fine and dandy".
- He also reads widely and drops references without explaining them: Fred Brooks, Peter Naur, Byron Sharp, Ehrenberg-Bass, Chesterton's fence, high modernism, Paul Graham, Komoroske, Brie Wolfson, Craig Mod.

### Humor
- Dry, deadpan, analogy-driven. Food and cooking metaphors keep coming back ("empty calories", "Where's the protein?", "home cooking your own software", "first ladel of piping hot goodness", "omakase designers").
- Sports analogies: "work like NFL players, playing one game a week for 17 weeks", "utility player".
- Remixed sayings: "'Half of the money I spend on tokens is wasted; the trouble is I don't know which half.'", "Friends don't let friends build roadmaps", "if you're so smart, why don't you ship?"
- Light mockery of tech culture: "tech bros inventing speed dating from first principles", "Big time NPC energy", "one-shotted by ayahuasca".

### Openings
- Blog: he opens with a quote or a link to someone else's post, then reacts ("Craig Mod writes:", "Alex Harri at Paper posted this slick demo...", "Brex shared the top 15 newsletters...").
- Or he opens with a current-events hook plus his contrarian frame: "The commentariat will have the world believing that Airtable is yet another victim of the AI revolution."
- Or with a personal anecdote: "Every so often, I stumble upon an interesting book in Amazon's recommendations..."
- No throat-clearing. From "Back in Action": "I will spare us all from the poetics about new blogs and just start writing."

### Closings
- A punchline or one-liner: "Where's the protein?", "To which I say, 'if we're so smart, why aren't we happy?'", "And nobody can do a damn thing about it."
- A rhetorical question: "But if it all becomes fake work, then it's not really your vocation, is it?"
- A prediction: "By early 2027, the industry will restructure around these realities, and sanity will return."
- Sometimes a casual hand-off link: "One more link for the road:", "I'll leave you with 100 use cases..."
- No "In conclusion", no "Let's dive in", no summary recaps.

### Lists and headings
- He uses lists rarely, and when he does they're short and parallel ("Here are 5 key traits of the omakase designer:", a 2-step method, a 5-item acquisition shortlist, a two-type dichotomy: "Designers that take orders / Designers that make magic").
- Most posts have no subheads. Short posts are untitled link-blog entries.
- The amoeba.design knowledge titles are casual and imperative or contrarian: "Don't be afraid of CSS", "Shake what the browser gave ya", "Cleaning up the Tailwind mess", "There's more to design engineering than microinteractions".

### Punctuation habits
- **Em dashes: almost never.** His amoebaunlimited posts contain 0 em dashes of his own. The only ones on the page come from quoted material. bryanking.net has exactly one ("That's not just faster—it's different."). Use commas, periods, parentheses, or ellipses instead.
- Parentheses for asides are frequent: "(it really is a merger, even though it's technically an acquisition)", "(that's software development lifecycle)", "(I haven't read)".
- Ellipses sometimes stand in for a dramatic pause: "...poor bets did.", "tear this place down to the studs…no, the foundation…no, the earth!"
- Exclamation points: occasional on the blog ("Exciting times in design tools!"). On X they're frequent, often doubled ("listen!!", "build lofi!!", "what the browser gives us!!").
- Scare quotes on industry buzzwords: "strategy", "distribution", "shift", "learning as we go", "launch".
- Emoji: essentially none. One 🤓 in about 120 posts, none on the blog.
- Capitalization: the blog uses normal sentence case. On X he often types all lowercase ("i read ~2,000 lines of agent-written code this week...", "lol i started a new css framework last week"). Use "lol" on X only.

### Recurring phrases and words
- "does what it says on the tin" (blog and X)
- "slop", "agent slop", "AI slop" ("Rage against the slop", "buff out the AI slop from your interface")
- "SDLC" (used a lot), "GTM", "PLG", "KTLO", "ZIRP", "ICP", "RFCs", "CI"
- "single player game" vs "MMORPG" (solo versus collaborative work)
- "go with the flow" / "status quo" (said with disdain)
- "one-shotted"
- "purist" ("scrum purist", "Shape Up purist", "CSS purist")
- "by the book" / "to a T" / "the handbook"
- "chops" ("front-end chops", "backend chops"), "taste", "judgment", "conceptual integrity"
- "ship" (constantly), "polished", "durable"
- "business 101" / "marketing 101"
- "the labs", "the punditry", "the commentariat", "tech zealots"
- "I'd wager", "I'm long on", "Hell,"

### Jargon level
- Medium-high for a technical B2B audience. He drops SDLC, GTM, PLG, ICP, KTLO, CI, PRs, RFCs, shadcn/ui, Tailwind, Storybook, MCP, and harness without defining them. Occasionally he glosses one in parentheses ("SDLC world (that's software development lifecycle)").
- He doesn't use marketing jargon ("synergy", "best-in-class", "end-to-end solutions"). He uses business-school ideas (core competencies, opportunity cost, brand growth) in plain words.

### How opinionated and contrarian
- Very. He frames many posts as a correction of the consensus: "AI didn't kill Airtable", "mobile-first mafia", "The times aren't a changin", "Against LGTM", "Product-led growth is probably going to die."
- He makes dated, falsifiable predictions ("figma will be the only dedicated design tool left in 24 months", "Summer of 2026 will go down as the real trough", "By early 2027...", "salesforce is going to acquire anthropic").
- He hedges honestly when he should ("I've not yet played with Paper, but...", "Maybe he'll come back around", "Or maybe I'm just a wacko who like to be a utility player?"). He's confident without being a know-it-all.

### How he talks about specific topics
- **AI:** He's a heavy, pragmatic user (Cursor daily since June 2024, cloud agents, Grok Bot) who is skeptical of the hype and the zealots. He calls bad output "slop". He insists on reading the code, he's pessimistic about software factories and one-person-billion-dollar narratives, and he's excited when one skilled person ships something real.
- **Design engineering:** He sees the job as owning the whole loop: design sense, front-end chops, enough backend, product judgment. On X he jokes that real design engineers write "1,500 word RFCs" to overhaul the design system and "add strict checks to the CI pipeline." His Knowledge article title says "There's more to design engineering than microinteractions."
- **CSS:** Affectionate purist. "CSS was easiest to grok bc it was like drawing or painting." "Mine is just one plain old css file." "it's time to use what the browser gives us!!" He credits CSS-Tricks for his career. He's down on long Tailwind class strings.
- **Design systems:** Enforcement and hygiene: shadcn/lint, failing CI on violations, removing agent overrides, Storybook docs for agents, Figma and code kept in sync through Cursor.
- **Clients and teams:** Practical and a bit impatient with teams that improvise process. He prefers scrum or Shape Up "by the book", limiting work in progress, staging environments, and open-source frameworks over homegrown stacks. He believes customers will tell you what to build. He dislikes calendar bloat and meetings about meetings.

## 3. Do / Don't for writers

**Do**
- Write in first person singular ("I"), as one craftsman talking to a founder or eng lead. Amoeba's own copy does this ("I'll ship polished UI as PRs").
- Lead with a concrete hook: a real problem ("Is your app rough around the edges?"), a number, a thing that happened, or a common belief you're about to correct.
- Keep sentences mostly short to medium. Use one-sentence paragraphs for punch. End sections on a one-liner.
- Use concrete specifics and numbers: "1,200 instances in a few days", "~2,000 lines of agent-written code", "5 days, one person", "$500 / week".
- Use a homespun or food/sports analogy at most once or twice per page ("buff out the AI slop", "home cooking your own software").
- Take a clear position and name the thing you're against (slop, overrides, roadmap theater, LGTM culture, roll-your-own stacks).
- Name real tools by name: Cursor, Figma, shadcn/ui, shadcn/lint, Tailwind, Storybook, CSS, the browser, PRs, CI.
- Use parentheses for asides and scare quotes for buzzwords you're poking at.
- Hedge where you honestly would ("I'd wager", "In my experience", "Maybe").
- On comparison pages, be blunt but fair. He gives credit where due ("Don't get me wrong, there's a lot of great stuff from Lenny and company.") before the knock.

**Don't**
- Don't use em dashes. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses.
- Don't use emoji, exclamation spam, or hashtags on website pages.
- Don't use the corporate "we" or agency puffery: "leverage", "synergy", "best-in-class", "world-class team", "cutting-edge", "seamless", "robust solutions", "empower", "unlock", "elevate", "supercharge your", "transform your business", "at Amoeba, we believe", "partner with us on your journey".
- Don't use AI-writing tells: "In today's fast-paced world", "Let's dive in", "It's not just X, it's Y" (he used it once, don't lean on it), "delve", "tapestry", "game-changer", "navigate the landscape", tidy three-part conclusions, or "In conclusion". He publicly dunks on synthetic text ("Not a whiff of AI generated text in here either. Bravo!!") and on Grok Bot's "zoomer slang slop".
- Don't hype AI as magic or promise "10x". His view is that AI amplifies skilled people, that you still read the code, and that slop is the enemy.
- Don't over-format: no walls of bullets, no H2 every 80 words, no bold on every other phrase. Lists should be short and parallel.
- Don't explain basic B2B or dev jargon to death. The audience is technical founders and eng leads.
- Don't copy his X register (all lowercase, "lol", profanity, "!!") into website pages. A mild "hell" or "damn" is fine in articles, but leave profanity off the offer page and checklist.
- Don't invent opinions. Echo the beliefs in section 6.

## 5. Long-form vs short-form, and which to use

| | Blog (long-form) | X (short-form) |
|---|---|---|
| Case | Normal sentence case | Often all lowercase |
| Profanity | Occasional, for emphasis | More frequent |
| Exclamation | Rare | Frequent, often "!!" |
| Structure | Hook, then argument with evidence and quotes, then punchline | One observation or prediction, sometimes plus a one-line kicker |
| Humor | Extended metaphors (hog butchering, omakase, crock pot) | Quips, remixed sayings, mockery |
| Evidence | Quotes, stats, books, links | Personal anecdotes, numbers from his own work |
| Hedging | Thoughtful ("I'd wager", "I suspect") | Bolder predictions, "like ive been saying" |

**For website pages, use the long-form blog voice as the base**, at the polish level of the amoeba.design homepage: sentence case, first person, short paragraphs, concrete numbers, one analogy, no emoji, no em dashes, profanity off. Take three things from X: the bluntness of the hook, the specifics from his own work (shadcn/lint, 1,200 overrides, PRs, CI), and the occasional quotable one-liner. For each page type:
- **Offer page:** match the amoeba.design copy. Short rhetorical questions that name the pain, then a plain promise in "I" voice, then price and scope stated flatly.
- **Comparison pages:** use the blog's contrarian-explainer mode ("AI didn't kill Airtable"). Correct the common assumption, give credit, then state the tradeoff plainly.
- **Articles:** open with a quote, link, or anecdote, make the argument, close on a one-liner. Bring the knowledge-article title style: casual, imperative, slightly cheeky.
- **Checklist tool:** terse imperatives in his words ("Read the code.", "Kill the overrides.", "Use what the browser gives you."). Use plain labels, not jokes, on every item.

## 6. Recurring public opinions (quote + source)
